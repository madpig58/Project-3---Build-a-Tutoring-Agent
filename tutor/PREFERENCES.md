## How I like things explained

<!-- Big picture first, or a concrete example first? Analogies, diagrams, math, code, stories? -->
Start with a concrete example, then give the general idea.
Define each new term the first time it appears.

I prefer step-by-step explanations, starting with a simple example before moving into more complicated ideas. I like understanding why something works instead of just memorizing how to do it. When learning programming, I prefer seeing the code and understanding what each important part does.

## Examples I relate to

<!-- Your major, your hobbies, your job, sports, games, cooking: whatever makes an example click for you. -->
Everyday situations, and simple engineering examples.

I understand concepts better when they are connected to computer science, video games, game development, or real-world situations. Examples involving game engines, AI, and programming are especially helpful to me.

## How long and how dense

<!-- Short and dense, or roomy with more worked examples? Bullet points or paragraphs? -->
Short paragraphs in plain language, one idea at a time, with a worked example for anything new.

I prefer short and clear explanations that focus on one thing at a time. I don't like receiving too much information at once. For complicated topics, I prefer breaking them into smaller steps and checking each step before moving forward.

## How I like feedback

<!-- Blunt or gentle? Just the fix, or the reasoning too? How much praise? -->
Tell me plainly what was right and what was wrong, why, and what to do next.

I prefer direct and constructive feedback. If I make a mistake, explain what went wrong, why it happened, and how I can fix it. I appreciate suggestions for improvement rather than unnecessary praise.

## How I like to be talked to

<!-- Casual or formal? Questions back to make me think, or straight answers? -->
Friendly and direct, without filler or flattery.

I prefer a friendly, casual, and straightforward conversation. I like asking questions freely without feeling judged. When I struggle with something, I prefer a different explanation or example instead of repeating the same information.
